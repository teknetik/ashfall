A reference sheet for 3D modelling of salvaged workshop materials from a desert colony repair bench. Six separate groups laid out on a flat neutral grey background in two rows of three, each group seen from a three-quarter view above, photographic studio product render, soft even light, no labels, no text captions, no hands, no tools. Everything is worn, salvaged, lightly dusty and physically plausible at real size.

1. Two salvaged scrap alloy sheets, each about 120 x 90 mm and 2.5 mm thick: aluminium alloy panel offcuts with old chipped industrial orange paint on one face, a fragment of white stencilled lettering, two old bolt holes, one torn ragged edge and scratches. One lies paint side up, the other bare side up showing brushed grey metal.
2. Pieces sheared from such a sheet: a narrow strap strip about 110 x 12 mm lying flat, the same strip bent in two places into a shallow U, several jagged offcuts with bright sheared edges, and a few curled swarf spirals from drilling.
3. Thin electrical leads, 1.6 mm thick with old dark red insulation, in stages side by side: one as cut with a pale marker band near its end; one trimmed shorter; one with 8 mm of insulation stripped and the copper strands twisted; one with the bare end tinned bright silver with solder.
4. A tiny axial trim resistor with colour bands and two wire legs; two short black heat-shrink sleeves; and a finished wire loop: two of the red leads soldered across the resistor with the two sleeves shrunk tight over the joints.
5. A small stoppered glass vial about 70 mm tall, two-thirds full of dull grey metallic fluid, with a scratched level line on the glass and a wired-on rubber stopper.
6. A small steel-and-wood wire bobbin about 35 mm across shown twice: empty, and wound full of bright enamelled copper wire.
