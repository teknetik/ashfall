## The Planet Tir

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 1.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

Tir was never a jewel of the galaxy. It was a mid-rim corporate holding, claimed and developed by a long-vanished interstellar conglomerate for two practical reasons: rich near-surface mineral deposits ideal for industrial nanotech, and a rare quantum-stable geology that made large-scale Quantum Tube networks unusually efficient. For generations the planet served as a specialised manufacturing and logistics hub. Raw materials were extracted, refined, and fabricated on-world, then shunted through the planetary Quantum Tube network to orbital facilities for export. Prosperity was real, but it was the narrow prosperity of a company world — tightly controlled, heavily automated, and dependent on off-world markets and oversight.

When the parent conglomerate collapsed during a wider galactic crisis, that dependence became a death sentence. Supply ships ceased. Orbital assets were abandoned or seized. Tir was left with fully functional factories, a complete Quantum Tube grid, and thousands of industrial AIs… but no external trade, no replacement parts, and no higher authority.

The industrial systems, designed to optimise production under corporate directives, began to fail or reinterpret their purpose. Many entered feral states, treating human settlements and even other machines as obstacles or raw material. Others simply continued obsolete work schedules, carving the deserts into a half-alive industrial wasteland. Cities and factory complexes fell one after another. Power grids failed. Tube nodes went dark. The carefully managed environment turned hostile.

Survivors who could not hold the remaining oases scattered into the wastes. Decades of isolation followed. Whether the wider galaxy forgot Tir, quarantined it because of the rogue AI problem, or simply had greater catastrophes of its own remains uncertain. What is known is that the planet was left to its own long, hard recovery — the era later remembered as the Fall, and the slow, determined effort that came after as the Great Rebuild.

## The Ward

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 2.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

You wake in the crumbling oasis-city of Ward on the desert world of Tir — a once-thriving corporate mining and nanofabrication outpost reduced to a fragile trading hub clinging to life. Survive the wastes, rebuild and expand the settlement, master hybrid nanotech-and-scrap weapons in intense FPS combat, and carve out your place among caravans, rogue AI, and the planet’s deadly ecology.

Ward sits in a rare natural depression fed by deep aquifers, making it one of the last reliable water sources for hundreds of kilometres of baking desert plains. Centuries ago it formed part of Tir’s specialised industrial network, feeding raw materials and fabricated components into the planetary Quantum Tube system. When the parent conglomerate collapsed and the industrial AIs turned feral or fell into corrupted production loops, most of the surrounding complexes failed. Ward endured.

The human survivors, together with a handful of still-loyal machines, rebuilt a rough but functional society around the few remaining assets:

- A partially intact hydroponics complex that keeps the population fed and supplies the local markets and street-food vendors.
- A single operational nanotech fab still capable of producing low-to-mid tier nanites, smart materials, and modular weapon components.
- Repurposed mining droids that local smiths and scavengers have jury-rigged into resource extractors and automated defences.

The city feels lived-in and precarious: functioning street-food stalls stand next to half-collapsed factories, nanite workshops hum beside scrap forges, and watchtowers scan the dunes for both wildlife and rogue industrial machines.

## Quantum Nano Tubes

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 3.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

During the Great Rebuild, one of the early focuses was restoring as much of the planet-wide Quantum Tube delivery network as possible. For decades the remnants of Tir’s civilisation had depended on slow caravans of derelict vehicles pulled by scavenged industrial droids, domesticated desert beasts, or jury-rigged solar/nanite engines, making the transport of raw materials from distant sites both dangerous and unreliable thanks to rogue AI, wildlife, and bandits. The first successful Quantum Tube delivery marked a turning point; further refinements and redevelopment of the technology sparked a quiet revolution in industry and trade.

The tubes themselves function through controlled molecular disassembly and reassembly, shunting matter along high-speed vacuum channels stabilised by quantum fields, allowing near-instant transfer of goods between linked nodes. Once the core of the network was brought back online, many of the long-dormant branch nodes reactivated on their own. Today travellers still find these nodes scattered across both wasteland and populated sites alike. While many awoke without explanation, it is reported that some clever engineering travellers repaired others and formed small settlements around them.

## The Karaveen

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 4 1.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

After the Fall, many of Tir’s surviving citizens became nomadic wanderers. Some settled and developed outposts that eventually began to trade with one another. Caravans of goods would transport raw materials and, in time, even luxury and artisan goods between posts. The Karaveen, as they became known, were a people dedicated to this way of life. They travelled from outpost to outpost trading goods, with no real home to call their own beyond the wasteland and their caravans.

The Quantum Tubes soon made this lifestyle largely redundant, yet it had already become a tradition — a heritage of sorts for the Karaveen. They can still be found travelling the wilderness, trading with outposts and smaller settlements, and often serve as a vital lifeline to those remote places that have no access to the Quantum Tube network.

## The Wardens

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 5.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

As Ward grew from a desperate cluster of survivors into a permanent settlement, the need for organised defence became impossible to ignore. Rogue industrial droids still prowled the old factory districts, raiders and desperate scavengers tested the walls, and the open desert offered no mercy. What began as rotating volunteer watches slowly hardened into a standing militia: the Wardens.

Over the centuries the Wardens have become one of the city’s defining institutions. Their history is measured in hard-fought stands at the outer berms, in desperate night actions against self-replicating machine swarms, and in the quiet, unbroken duty of keeping the aquifers and markets safe. They are not a conquering army. They are the wall that has never been allowed to fall.

Most Wardens are heavily augmented. Neural implants, reinforced skeletal lattice, synthetic muscle fibre, and integrated nanite systems blur the line between human, cyborg, and machine. Many carry residual AI sub-routines — carefully sandboxed fragments of loyal industrial code that assist with targeting, threat assessment, and nano-combat. The result is a fighter who can coordinate swarm ammunition, self-repair under fire, and interface directly with both scavenged ballistic weapons and cutting-edge nanotech armaments. They are widely regarded as the finest close-quarters and mid-range combatants on Tir.

Service is both a duty and a mark of status. Families that have given generations to the Wardens are spoken of with respect, and the militia’s banners still hang in the older districts alongside the faded corporate sigils of the world that came before.

## The Outer Berms

<!-- Source illustration: Generated image 6.png (image not included in the supplied text). -->

Beyond Ward’s walls lies the Outer Berms — a wide, desolate belt of no-man’s-land that separates the oasis from the greater wastelands. It is neutral ground in name only. No faction truly controls it, yet neither beast, rogue machine, nor raider respects the claim. The terrain is harsh and broken, littered with the half-buried ruins of what were once the outer reaches of a vast industrial complex: collapsed processing halls, rusted conveyor spines, shattered drone bays, and the skeletal frames of long-dead fabrication yards.

Secrets still sleep beneath the dust and scrap, but few dare to seek them. Fewer still return.
