# Presentation and camera follow-up audit — 26 September 2026

Read-only review found no additional demonstrated regression requiring a source change after the saved shadow-proxy reparent. The camera and presentation visual use the same `PlayerMotor.RenderPosition` calculation, independent of their LateUpdate ordering. Physics and interactions retain the current controller root. Teleports reset both history samples; disable restores the authored visual pose; scaled pause freezes presentation time. The expected one-fixed-step presentation delay remains 20 ms, up to 12 cm at the configured 6 m/s run speed.

First person derives its eye from that same presented position. The collision root remains current, so a moving player's snapshot eye-to-root offset can include the intentional interpolation delay; compare the stationary endpoint or the presented position when validating alignment. At zero boom the camera uses the 1.65 m eye and skips the boom sweep. Close camera modes switch the cached descendant renderers to ShadowsOnly and restore their original modes afterward. The capsule proxy is now among those same presentation descendants, and has no collider. The saved actual body already casts its own shadow, so proxy redundancy remains a separate visual decision.

The existing third-person sweep has a known conditional limitation: a sphere cast does not detect a collider already overlapping its starting sphere. Unity documents this in the [installed-version SphereCast reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/ScriptReference/Physics.SphereCast.html). The current camera has no separate initial-overlap depenetration path. This is a risk to reproduce around a tight corner or after an invalid teleport, not evidence of a newly introduced clipping failure. Do not add speculative collision behavior without a saved-scene reproduction. The runtime overlap snapshot uses radius 0.20 m, while the camera sweep uses 0.23 m; a passing snapshot is not a full equivalence test of the sweep.

Remaining native checks are moving first-person/third-person transitions, walls and doorways, stairs, jumping and landing, direction reversals, and pause/resume. Verify physical clearance and inspect the rendered result. Fixed-step traces on a slow isolated software renderer can check jump/state logic, but cannot qualify camera smoothness, animation contact or temporal stability at the target frame rate. No source or scene changes were made for this audit.
