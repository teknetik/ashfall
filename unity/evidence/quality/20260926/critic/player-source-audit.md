# Player portrait: retained source versus runtime — 26 September 2026

**The available evidence points to a source-detail limitation, not a discarded high-detail player model.** The current scene uses the supplied player's full topology and its original 4096 × 4096 color image. There is no supplied normal, roughness or metallic map to recover. The rejected angular head, solid hair/beard silhouette and weak material separation therefore need a deliberately authored derivative; raising a texture cap or rerunning the importer cannot supply missing structure.

This was a read-only audit while native performance diagnostics were running. Required records `unity/MESHY_PLAYER.md` and `docs/model-import.md` were read first. No Unity/Blender invocation, geometry binary loading, asset modification or new runtime capture was performed. GLB inspection read only JSON chunks and 24-byte PNG headers. ## Material and head limitations

The scene's `Assets/AthenHill/Art/ActorSurfaceFidelity/Player.mat` binds the original embedded albedo, with metallic **0**, smoothness **0.25**, no normal map, no packed metallic/smoothness texture and no occlusion map. Skin, hair, fabric and armour consequently share one uniform surface response, even where the color suggests different materials. This is a confirmed material limitation. It does not establish that the entire armour should be metallic: worn coatings, cloth, leather and bare metal require different semantic regions.

The original export uses the same image for full-strength emission and base color. The current recovery correctly disables that full-body emission. Restoring the original emission would wash out lighting and would not recover realistic skin or armour. Cyan details should receive emission only if a separately authored semantic mask justifies it.

The source has no morph targets. Its 24-joint skeleton is retained; a facial animation system is not hidden in the material/importer. The source idle/talk note in `MESHY_PLAYER.md` is historical: the current `MeshyPlayer.prefab` references later authored idle/talk and jump clips alongside supplied locomotion, so rerunning the original installer could overwrite later motion work. Do not use that installer as a portrait repair.

The current portrait proves an objectionable result, but this audit does **not** isolate how much of the cheek faceting comes from coarse geometry, preserved vertex normals or painted shading. The binary vertex data, head wireframe, UV occupancy and original color image were not loaded. A neutral clay/normal audition after profiling is the small next diagnostic, not an assumption that global smoothing fixes anatomy. Original per-material extensions are listed as importer report items, but the scene now uses the explicit URP Lit recovery material; extension warnings do not explain a missing high-detail face.

## Bounded next intervention

1. Preserve the original GLB, player prefab, skeleton, body proportions, UVs and authored clips. Create a separate head-surface candidate and a reversible renderer/material assignment. Inspect the existing head under neutral material and the current albedo, from portrait and side views, to distinguish normals from actual silhouette and baked shading.
2. Author skin/hair/beard versus armour/fabric regions for measured roughness and, where justified, shallow normal detail. Keep dielectric skin/hair nonmetallic. A semantic mask can improve separation without changing identity or the rig, but it cannot repair an angular ear, jaw or beard silhouette. Do not derive deep normals or metal simply from albedo brightness.
3. If the clay view confirms coarse head/hair shapes, restrict geometry work to that region: preserve recognisable face proportions and bone bindings, refine the head planes and hair/beard boundary, and bake an authored detailed surface onto a near mesh. Hair strand/card breakup is a possible bounded derivative, not a claim that adding arbitrary cards or subdivision produces realism. Keep the current body and locomotion intact and compare a full-body view as well as portrait/side close-ups.

For this focused pass, the priority is the solid beard/hair silhouette and broad face planes; fine pore detail follows rather than conceals them. Test a head material candidate separately before combining it with geometry. Any intended authored derivative needs a distinct provenance/validation path: the existing fidelity helper intentionally rejects changes away from its albedo-only, exact-source contract.

Remaining verification: actual player resident mip/compression in the native close-up; head UV density; whether preserved normals cause some visible facets; animation deformation after a derivative; sun/shade material response; and native cost. No source or runtime portrait quality has been accepted as AAA, and the available official ARK environment references do not provide a matched human-face comparison.
